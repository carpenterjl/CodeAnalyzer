## 4. Wrong answers

**`get_callers` on `OptionSpec` returned 22 referencing methods. The real figure is ≥41 files.**

- **Confidence it wore:** none stated. A clean list of 22 methods with file:line, formatted
  identically to a complete answer. Nothing in the output referred back to the 136 imperfect
  parses that `stats` and `parse_errors` report.
- **What I nearly shipped:** a plan asserting "only 22 call sites across ~99 builtin files, so
  most option handling is hand-rolled." The opposite is true — centralisation is roughly
  two-thirds — and the architectural conclusion drawn from it would have been backwards.
- **Cause, measured rather than assumed:** the construct being counted is written with a C# 12
  collection expression, which is exactly what the bundled grammar stops at:

  ```csharp
  new OptionSpec(name, Flags: ["rows", "sorted", "stable"], Names: [], StringPositionals: 2)
  ```

  **39 of the 41 files that mention `OptionSpec` are also in the `parse_errors` list — 95%. The
  only two that are not are the two that define it** (`JgsBuiltins.Options.cs`,
  `JgsBuiltins.Imaging.Options.cs`). The truncation and the undercount are the same event.

**`get_symbol` on the `JgsType` enum reported `callers: 4`** for an enum switched on throughout a
5,227-line interpreter. Same shape of error, same lack of any hedge. I did not chase the cause
here, so I record it as an observation rather than ranked grounds.

*I am deliberately not filing "136 files have syntax errors" — that is the documented known
limit. What I am filing is that the limit is reported in `stats` and `errors` and then silently
dropped by the query that consumes it.*

## 5. Pitfalls

- **`search_symbols` fuzzy mode is noisy on short queries.** `OptionSpec` ranked test-method
  names like `Quiver_AppliesOptionsAndALineSpec` and `Microstrip_SynthesisThenAnalysis_RoundTripsTheImpedance`
  alongside the real record — subsequence matching, working as designed, but it made me read
  carefully to find the one line I wanted. `exact: true` exists and I should have reached for it.
- **It is not semantic.** `handle property table` returned nothing; `file_outline` on the right
  file answered the same question instantly. My mistake, and a discoverability note: the natural
  first instinct on an unfamiliar repo is to describe rather than name.
- **The header says "call reindex to refresh" immediately after a successful `reindex`.** The
  first `stats` after building the index read `997 indexed files unchanged on disk — call reindex
  to refresh`, which reads as staleness when nothing is stale. (Later in the session it correctly
  said `3 of 997 indexed files changed on disk`, so the phrasing is only misleading in the
  nothing-changed case.)
- **My own worst misreading:** I took `callers: 22` as a completeness claim when it is a count of
  what the index holds. The tool never said the index was complete — it said so in two other
  places I had already read.

## 6. What I would fix, ranked

| # | Fix | Grounds (measured) | Query that measured it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Carry the parse-truncation warning into per-symbol answers.** When `get_callers`/`get_symbol` report counts for a symbol whose referencing files are truncated, say so in that output — "N of the M files referencing this were truncated; this count is a lower bound." | **39 of 41** files mentioning `OptionSpec` are in `parse_errors` (95%); the reported caller count was **22** against **≥41** files. Across the corpus, **136 of 997** files (13.6%) are truncated and **119** stop at `[]`. | `grep -rl OptionSpec src/JGraph.Scripting/Jgs/` intersected with the `parse_errors` file list; `get_callers` on `#30638`; `parse_errors --limit 200`. |
| 2 | **A way to ask for every reference to a type regardless of enclosing scope.** The dominant idiom here is a class-level field initialiser, and `get_callers` appears to report method-scope references. | **17** of the references are `new OptionSpec(`; **196** mentions total across **41** files; `get_callers` returned **22**. The gap between 22 and 196 is not all truncation — the idiom is `private static readonly OptionSpec X = new(...)`, a field, and **every one of the 196 mentions is the bare token `OptionSpec`** (no qualified spellings to confuse matching). | `grep -rho "[A-Za-z_.]*OptionSpec[A-Za-z_]*" \| sort \| uniq -c` → `196 OptionSpec`; `grep -rc "new OptionSpec("` → 17. |
| 3 | **Upgrade the bundled C# grammar to one that reads collection expressions.** This is the root cause of #1 rather than a mitigation of it. | **119 of 136** imperfect parses stop at `[]` — 87.5% of all parse failures in the corpus, and 12% of all C# files. Truncation points are early in large files: `JgsBuiltins.Statistics.Cluster.cs` stops at **line 27**, `JgsBuiltins.Imaging.Color.cs` at **18**, `Interpreter.cs` at **30**. | `parse_errors` tally block. |
| 4 | **Suppress "call reindex to refresh" when nothing changed.** Cosmetic, but it undermines trust in every header that follows. | Observed on the first `stats` after a successful `reindex`: `997 indexed files unchanged on disk — call reindex to refresh`. | `reindex` then `stats`, same session. |

I considered ranking the `JgsType` `callers: 4` result and did not, because I did not measure its
cause and it is plausibly the same bug as #1 or the same design as #2. Ranking it separately
would be a third claim from one observation.

## Verdict

**It earned its place, and it also cost me an hour I would not have lost to grep.** `file_outline`
and `get_callees` were strictly better than grep for "what does this file define" and "who owns
this call" — the property-table design fell out of one call and became an axis of the audit, and
`get_symbol` on `JgsType` corrected a belief I had taken from a planning document. Against that,
the one question where I trusted a count instead of reading it produced a confident wrong number
about the exact construct the codebase uses everywhere, and only a grep caught it. On a modern-C#
corpus the failure mode to watch is not refusal — it is a quiet undercount that looks like an
answer.
